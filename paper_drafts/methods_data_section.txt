--- 3. Data and Study Area ---

3.1  Crop and Market Scope

This study covers three essential horticultural commodities — tomato
(Solanum lycopersicum), onion (Allium cepa), and potato (Solanum
tuberosum) — collectively known as TOP crops, which together account
for approximately 30% of India's total vegetable production by volume
and exhibit characteristically high price volatility due to their
perishable nature, limited cold-chain penetration, and concentration
of production in a small number of states (Kumar et al., 2021).

3.2  Primary Data Source: Agmarknet Weekly Price Panel

Weekly wholesale price and arrivals data were obtained from the
Agricultural Marketing Information Network (Agmarknet), the Government
of India's real-time electronic market reporting platform that covers
over 7,000 Agricultural Produce Market Committee (APMC) mandis across
all states and union territories. Raw transaction-level data spanning
January 2000 to April 2026 were downloaded for all three crops; the
analysis period was restricted to ISO calendar weeks 1–52/53 of
2017–2024 (418 weeks in total), capturing two complete agricultural
price cycles (2017–2020 and 2021–2024) as well as the COVID-19
disruption period.

Price records were aggregated to the market-week level using the
arrivals-weighted mean of daily modal prices:

    P_{m,t} = Σ_d (p_{m,d} × q_{m,d}) / Σ_d q_{m,d}

where p_{m,d} is the modal price (Rs/quintal) and q_{m,d} is the
arrivals volume (tonnes) on day d of ISO week t for market m.
Weekly arrivals were summed across all days within each ISO week.
Markets reporting zero or negative arrivals in a given week were
excluded from that week's computation but retained in the panel.

After aggregation, the raw panel contained 1,567 tomato markets,
843 onion markets, and 85 potato markets across 29, 22, and 2 states
respectively. The notably smaller potato sample reflects the highly
concentrated production geography of the crop (Uttar Pradesh and
West Bengal account for >60% of national output) and the limited
number of regulated APMCs active in those states during the study
period.

3.3  Market Selection Criterion: Temporal Coverage Threshold

A key challenge in any long-run panel study of Indian agricultural
markets is incomplete temporal coverage: APMC reporting frequency
varies substantially by state, season, and crop, resulting in many
markets with long gaps or very sparse participation in the Agmarknet
system. Including markets with low coverage inflates the panel but
introduces structural missingness that degrades feature computation
(particularly lag and rolling-window features) and produces
unreliable model estimates.

For each market m and crop c, we computed the temporal coverage index:

    TC_{m,c} = W_{m,c} / W_total × 100

where W_{m,c} is the number of ISO weeks in 2017–2024 for which
market m reported at least one valid price observation, and W_total =
418 is the total number of ISO weeks in the study period.

Markets were retained in the analytical panel only if TC_{m,c} ≥ 90%
(i.e., present in at least 376 of 418 weeks). This threshold was
selected to balance two competing objectives: (i) ensuring that
time-series feature vectors (up to 52-week lags, 13-week rolling
windows) are computed over complete sequences without structural gaps,
and (ii) preserving sufficient market diversity across states and
production regions to allow the model to generalise.

Application of the 90% threshold yielded a high-quality balanced panel
of 286 tomato markets (19 states), 123 onion markets (11 states), and
64 potato markets (2 states), containing 115,413, 49,178, and 25,888
market-week observations respectively (Table 1). The resulting panel
spans 190,479 observations in total. Among the selected markets, median
temporal coverage exceeded 97% for all three crops, and the minimum
was 90% by construction. The coverage distribution by state is shown
in Figure 3; major producing states — Karnataka, Maharashtra, and
Andhra Pradesh for tomato; Maharashtra, Karnataka, and Rajasthan for
onion; and Uttar Pradesh for potato — are well-represented.

3.4  Macro-Economic and Logistics Covariates (Layer 4)

Monthly macro-economic indicators were obtained from three sources
and aligned to the weekly panel by matching on (year, month):

(a) CMIE Prowess database: agricultural credit outstanding
    (Rs crore), vegetable export and import values (USD million),
    crude oil Indian Basket price (USD/barrel), Index of Industrial
    Production for food processing (base 2011-12), and agricultural
    wages (Rs/day).

(b) RBI Database on the Indian Economy (DBIE): repo rate (%),
    reverse repo rate (%), monthly average USD/INR exchange rate,
    Wholesale Price Index for all commodities, food articles, and
    fruits and vegetables (base 2011-12).

(c) Petroleum Planning & Analysis Cell (PPAC): retail diesel price
    at Delhi (Rs/litre) and LPG price (Rs/14.2 kg cylinder),
    used as a proxy for transportation and cold-storage cost.

All macro series cover January 2017–December 2024 (96 monthly
observations). Monthly values were broadcast to the weekly level
by matching each ISO week to the month containing its start date.
A total of 14 macro variables were retained after removing series
with >5% missingness or near-zero variance (see Table A1 in
Supplementary Material).

3.5  Satellite and Climate Data (Layers 2 & 3)

Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery (10 m, Level-2A) was accessed via
the Google Earth Engine (GEE) cloud platform for 14 major production
districts identified within the ≥90% coverage market panel. For each
district, the Enhanced Vegetation Index (EVI) and Normalised
Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) were extracted at the
pre-harvest phenological window (6–12 weeks before expected peak
harvest) and aggregated to ISO-week composites. Values with |EVI| > 2
were masked as cloud contamination artefacts. Weekly NDVI anomaly
(departure from the 2017–2024 district-level weekly mean) was
computed as the primary satellite predictor. Climate stress indices
(Standardised Precipitation Index at 30- and 90-day timescales,
weekly maximum temperature, heat-degree-days above 35°C, and
cumulative excess-rainfall days) were derived from ERA5-Land hourly
reanalysis data (0.1° × 0.1° grid) accessed via the Copernicus
Climate Data Store and spatially averaged over each production
district's bounding box.

3.6  Data Consistency and Quality Assessment

Figure 4 (year-by-year coverage heatmap) illustrates the temporal
completeness of the top 30 markets per crop. Notably, the COVID-19
lockdown period (March–June 2020) caused a visible dip in reporting
across all crops, most pronounced for tomato markets in Karnataka and
Andhra Pradesh. Markets with recoverable gaps (single isolated weeks)
were not imputed; instead, the feature engineering pipeline was
designed to tolerate short gaps by computing rolling statistics only
over non-null observations within the window. Markets where the
lockdown gap exceeded four consecutive weeks retained ≥90% annual
coverage in all other years and thus passed the 90% threshold at the
full-period level.

Figure 5 (weekly panel density timeline) shows the count of
market-weeks reporting prices in each ISO week from 2017 to 2024.
The overall trend shows increasing participation over time,
reflecting the expansion of the Agmarknet digital reporting system.
The filtered (≥90%) panel shows a stable and dense baseline across
all years, confirming that the threshold effectively removes
sporadically reporting mandis while preserving the bulk of
consistently active markets.
