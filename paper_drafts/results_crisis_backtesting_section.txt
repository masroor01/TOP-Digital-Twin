--- X. Crisis Backtesting: Case Studies of Extreme Price Events ---

X.1  Motivation and Episode Selection

The ablation study (Section 5) and the Model Confidence Set analysis
(Section 5.X) establish that naive persistence is the statistically
confirmed best-performing model at the shortest forecast horizon
(h = 1 week) for all three crops, with the full data-layer pipeline
(M6) becoming statistically indistinguishable from, or superior to,
naive persistence only from the medium-to-long horizons (h = 13-26
weeks) onward. These results are averages over four non-overlapping
annual test folds (2022-2025) and do not, on their own, indicate
whether the same ranking holds during the extreme price events that
are of greatest practical interest to policymakers. We therefore
conducted four targeted backtests of real, historically documented
price crises, using the already-computed out-of-sample fold
predictions from the ablation study (no models were retrained for
this analysis, preserving the strict train/test separation of the
original rolling-origin design).

Episodes were selected using two independent criteria in
combination: (i) the four largest four-week percentage price moves
(both spikes and crashes) identified empirically in the real,
non-imputed weekly panel within the 2022-2025 out-of-sample window,
and (ii) cross-referencing against a verified, primary-source-cited
log of Government of India export-policy and market-intervention
events (export bans, minimum export price notifications, export
duty changes, buffer procurement, and Operation Greens/Market
Intervention Scheme actions). This combination ensures the episodes
are not cherry-picked from the model's own results, but are instead
the panel's own largest documented moves, with a traceable policy
record where one exists. Four episodes met these criteria:

  (a) Tomato, June-September 2023: a nationwide spike of +454% over
      four weeks (peak week of 3 July 2023) followed by a -70% crash
      within six weeks, coinciding with a National Cooperative
      Consumers' Federation (NCCF) and National Agricultural
      Cooperative Marketing Federation (NAFED) subsidised retail
      intervention beginning 20 July 2023.
  (b) Onion, August 2023-January 2024: a sustained price escalation
      (+89% over four weeks at peak, late October 2023) tracking a
      sequence of increasingly restrictive export measures -- a 40%
      export duty (19 August 2023), a minimum export price of USD
      800/MT (28 October 2023), and an outright export ban (8
      December 2023) -- followed by a -46% crash within three weeks
      of the ban taking effect.
  (c) Potato, April 2024: a nationwide spike of +45% over four weeks,
      with no corresponding entry in the verified policy-event log.
  (d) Potato, January 2025: a nationwide crash of -47% over four
      weeks, likewise with no corresponding policy-event record.

For each episode, forecast errors (MAE, MAPE) for the M6 (full
pipeline) and naive-persistence baselines were computed over a
"core" crisis window bracketing the price extreme (Table X.1),
restricted in every case to predictions drawn from a fold whose
training cutoff strictly precedes the episode, so that no model saw
the crisis during training.

Two patterns are consistent across all four episodes and merit
separate discussion: the short-horizon result, which replicates the
aggregate ablation finding without exception, and the long-horizon
result, which sharpens it considerably.

X.2.1  Short horizon (h = 1 week): naive persistence is undefeated

Naive persistence outperforms M6 at h = 1 week in every one of the
four episodes, including during the most extreme single-week moves
in the entire dataset. This is consistent with, and reinforces, the
aggregate Model Confidence Set finding (Section 5.X) that naive
persistence is the sole statistically defensible model at this
horizon for all three crops under normal conditions. Two mechanisms
plausibly explain why this advantage persists, rather than reverses,
during a crisis. First, price is strongly autocorrelated at a
one-week lag even during rapid movements, since a spike or crash is
by construction a multi-week process; the naive forecast, which
copies the most recently observed real price, tracks a fast-moving
level more tightly than a model attempting to extrapolate structural
features. Second, gradient-boosted tree ensembles such as LightGBM
cannot extrapolate beyond the range of values observed during
training (Ke et al., 2017); when the 3 July 2023 tomato price (Rs
7,227/quintal) substantially exceeded any value present in the M6
model's training folds, the one-week model was structurally unable
to predict a comparably extreme value, whereas naive persistence --
which simply repeats the latest real trade price -- faced no such
constraint.

X.2.2  Medium-to-long horizon (h = 13-26 weeks): the data layers
       earn their value precisely where naive persistence fails

At h = 13 and h = 26 weeks, M6 outperforms naive persistence in
three of the four episodes, in each case by a wide margin: a
2.6-7.5-fold reduction in MAPE for tomato, a 1.7-2.7-fold reduction
for onion, and a 2.0-2.7-fold reduction for the potato crash. Naive
persistence's long-horizon error during these episodes is not merely
larger but categorically different in character -- MAPE exceeding
100% during the tomato episode (146.7-157.9%) indicates the naive
forecast is, on average, wrong by more than the price level itself.
This follows directly from the definition of the naive forecast: a
prediction made 13 or 26 weeks in advance is simply the price
observed at that earlier date, carried forward without adjustment.
For a crisis with a genuine onset -- as opposed to a continuation of
an already-elevated price regime -- this anchor necessarily predates
the event, so naive persistence is mechanically incapable of
anticipating it at any lead time exceeding the event's own duration.
The M6 pipeline, by contrast, has access to climate-stress,
satellite-vegetation, arrivals, and -- for onion specifically --
explicit policy-instrument features (export-ban status, minimum
export price level, export duty rate) that permit the model to
condition its long-horizon forecast on information correlated with,
or directly constitutive of, the eventual price response, even when
that response has not yet begun at the time the forecast is issued.

The onion episode offers the most direct evidence for this
mechanism. The M6 feature set includes the same export-ban, minimum-
export-price, and export-duty variables that were used to construct
the intervention sequence in Table X.1's description, meaning the
model's long-horizon forecasts during this episode had access, in
principle, both to the contemporaneous state of trade policy and to
the learned historical relationship between comparable past
interventions and subsequent price movements. The crossover to M6
superiority occurs by h = 13 weeks during this crisis, one horizon
step earlier than the h = 26-week crossover identified in the full-
sample average for onion (Section 5.X), indicating that the
data layers' advantage over naive persistence is not merely
statistically confirmed on average but is concentrated during the
crisis windows in which accurate medium-term forecasting carries the
greatest policy value -- for example, in timing a minimum-export-
price revision or an export-duty decision with sufficient lead time
to affect the market before, rather than after, a price spike peaks.

X.2.3  Potato: a directionally asymmetric exception

Potato is the one crop for which the medium-to-long-horizon
crossover does not hold uniformly, and the asymmetry between its two
episodes is itself an informative result rather than a uniform
weakness. During the April 2024 spike, naive persistence outperforms
M6 at every horizon tested, including h = 26 weeks, where M6's MAPE
(48.2%) is nearly double naive persistence's (28.5%). This is
independent corroboration, via a genuinely different form of
evidence, of the Model Confidence Set finding that potato at h = 26
weeks is the one crop-horizon combination in which neither naive
persistence nor the full M6 pipeline survives the 90% confidence set
-- only the simpler price-and-arrivals variants (M0, M1) do. During
the January 2025 crash, however, M6 outperforms naive persistence
from h = 4 weeks onward by margins comparable to tomato and onion
(a 2.0-2.7-fold MAPE reduction at h = 13-26 weeks).

A plausible explanation for this asymmetry, offered here as a
hypothesis for future investigation rather than a confirmed finding,
is that potato's price crashes are predominantly supply-side and
storage-driven -- governed by cold-storage release timing and
harvest-season stock levels, which the model's infrastructure and
seasonal features are reasonably well positioned to capture -- while
its spikes are more likely driven by acreage or crop-damage shocks
localised to specific production districts, a signal that
national- or zone-level aggregated satellite and climate covariates
may not resolve with sufficient spatial precision. Unlike tomato and
onion, potato has no active export-control or minimum-export-price
regime in the study period (Section X.1; no corresponding
policy-event record exists for either potato episode), removing one
of the two mechanisms identified above as driving the data layers'
long-horizon advantage in the other two crops.

X.3  Summary

Naive persistence's short-horizon advantage, identified in the
aggregate ablation and Model Confidence Set results, is not an
artefact of averaging over calm and volatile periods: it holds, without
exception, during the four largest price movements in the study
period. The full data-layer pipeline's advantage at longer horizons,
however, is not merely preserved during these episodes but amplified
-- naive persistence's structural inability to anticipate a genuine
regime change produces its largest errors of the entire study
specifically during a crisis, while the data layers (climate,
satellite, arrivals, and policy features) allow the model to
partially anticipate the event from a considerable lead time. This
result reframes the study's central finding: the ablation study's
data layers are not merely statistically confirmed to add value on
average at long horizons, they add the most value precisely when a
policymaker would most need it. Potato is the exception that proves
informative rather than merely negative -- its crash dynamics behave
like tomato's and onion's, while its spike dynamics do not, pointing
to a specific, falsifiable hypothesis (localised crop-damage shocks
under-resolved by national-scale satellite covariates) for future
work rather than a blanket limitation of the modelling approach.
